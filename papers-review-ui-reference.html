3.2 Data and knowledge extraction

The empirical analysis compares publications in artificial intelligence (AI) and neurosciences (NS) over the period 2000–2023. The data-construction procedure combines journal classification, bibliographic retrieval, and document-level filtering.

We first identified first-quartile (Q1) journals in the “Computer Science, Artificial Intelligence” and “Neurosciences” categories from the 2024 Journal Citation Reports (JCR) in the Web of Science (WoS) database. We then retrieved all publications published in these journals between 2000 and 2023 from OpenAlex, using the CWTS-processed structured version of the OpenAlex snapshot released on August 30, 2024, and matched them by ISSNs and journal names. After excluding non-English publications, duplicate records, and records with missing metadata, the final sample contained 133,388 papers from 51 AI Q1 journals and 266,269 papers from 77 NS Q1 journals.

4.3 Validation of the network-coupling method

The edge-coupling method proposed by Ba et al. has been validated in prior studies of undirected science–technology knowledge network coupling. Because the semantic triple networks in this study are directed networks, we further tested whether this method remains suitable for directed semantic network comparison. We compared the edge-coupling method with ASD and QAP.

Fig. coupling-algorithm-comparison shows that the edge-coupling method achieves an average TCS above 0.9 across nearly all network scales while requiring less computation time than the comparison methods. The balance between ability to distinguish network structures and computational efficiency justifies our selection of the method proposed by Ba et al. as the edge-coupling method used in this study.
